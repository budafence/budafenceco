Austin Fence Permit Guide: Everything You Need to Know in 2026

By Buda Fence Company  ·  7 min read

Building a fence in Austin, Texas can be a great way to enhance your property's privacy, security, and curb appeal. But before you start your project, it's crucial to understand the city's fence permit requirements. This guide walks you through everything you need to know about Austin's fence permit regulations in 2026.

When Do You Need a Fence Permit in Austin?

One of the most common questions Austin homeowners ask is whether they need a permit to build a fence. According to the City of Austin, a fence requires a permit if it meets any of the following criteria:

  • Is located in a floodplain: If your property is in a designated floodplain, you will need a permit for any fence construction. Check the City of Austin's Floodpro webpage to verify your property's status.
  • Is more than 7 feet high at any point: Any fence exceeding 7 feet in height requires a permit — even if only a small section exceeds the limit.
  • Is more than 6 feet high along a public right-of-way: If your fence is adjacent to a sidewalk or street and exceeds 6 feet, a permit is required. This does not apply to fences along a public alley.

Even if your fence doesn't require a permit, it must still comply with all applicable city codes and regulations covering materials, construction practices, and property line setbacks.

What is Regulated?

  • Fence Height: Specific height restrictions determine when a permit is needed. Limits vary depending on location on your property.
  • Materials: The city prohibits barbed wire, electrified wire, and other hazardous materials.
  • Setbacks: Rules govern how close your fence can be to property lines, sidewalks, and streets.
  • Visibility at Intersections: Fences near street corners must not obstruct driver sightlines — the "visibility triangle" rule.

What is Not Regulated?

  • Neighbor disputes: The city does not get involved in disagreements between property owners about shared fences.
  • Deed restrictions: Your HOA may have its own rules separate from city regulations. Always check with your HOA before building.

Safe Fencing Requirements

The Austin City Council passed updated "Safe Fencing" requirements that apply to all new fences and some existing fences. Key requirements include:

  • Prohibition of hazardous features: Sharp pickets, pointed tops, and closely spaced vertical bars that can trap a child's head are prohibited.
  • Wildlife-friendly design: Regulations encourage gaps at the bottom of fences and materials that won't entangle animals.

Other Regulations That Affect Fence Design

  • Swimming pool and spa code: Pool fences must be at least 4 feet high with a self-closing, self-latching gate. We install pool-compliant metal fences that meet all Austin codes.
  • Utility easements: You cannot build a fence that blocks access to utility easements on your property.

How to Apply for a Fence Permit

  1. Gather required documents: You'll need a site plan showing the fence location, height, and materials, plus a property survey.
  2. Complete the application form: The residential fence permit application is available on the City of Austin's website.
  3. Submit the application and pay the fee: You can submit online or in person. Fees vary based on project scope.
  4. Schedule an inspection: After permit approval, schedule an inspection to confirm the fence matches approved plans.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a permit to repair an existing fence?

In most cases no — as long as repairs don't change the height or location. However, if you're replacing more than 50% of the fence, you may need a new permit.

How close to my property line can I build a fence?

Setback requirements vary by zoning and location. Consult a surveyor or the City of Austin's Development Services Department for your specific property.

What happens if I build a fence without a permit?

You may face fines and be required to remove or modify the fence to bring it into compliance. It's always better to confirm permit requirements before breaking ground.
